Standardising Paediatric Medicine Labels to Enhance Safety and Clarity

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 23 June 2025
  • 1:27 min
  • ST
    Steve TomlinDirector of the Children’s Medicines Research & Innovation Centre at Great Ormond Street Hospital

Steve Tomlin, Director of the Children’s Medicines Research & Innovation Centre at Great Ormond Street Hospital, discusses the challenges and solutions around the labelling of paediatric medicines. The video highlights the significant variability in how doses are written on pharmacy labels—some use milligrams, others millilitres, and some a combination of both—leading to confusion for parents and carers. Steve explains that while legal requirements for pharmacy labelling exist, the level of detail is not specified, resulting in inconsistent practices. He emphasises that the most important factor is consistency: all labels should be written the same way, regardless of the chosen format. However, research shows that parents prefer doses to be written in millilitres, as this is the unit they use when administering the medicine. Steve details ongoing efforts to standardise paediatric medicine labels, including a forthcoming position statement from the Neonatal and Paediatric Pharmacy Group, and discussions with regulators to establish national standards. The primary recommendation is that all liquid medicines should display the dose in millilitres, simplifying administration and reducing the risk of errors. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.
