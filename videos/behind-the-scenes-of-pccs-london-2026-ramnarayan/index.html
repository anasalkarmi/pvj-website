Behind the scenes of PCCS London 2026

  • The scientific programme aimed to bring speakers from a variety of backgrounds and to showcase the breadth of expertise across West London.
  • Events management support covered abstract management, AV, the venue, sponsors and charity partner, and eight volunteers helped the conference run smoothly.

Professor Padmanabhan Ramnarayan, Professor of Paediatric Critical Care at Imperial College London, reflects on organising PCCS London 2026 at Lord’s. He discusses choosing the venue, developing the scientific programme and bringing together a broad range of expertise. The interview explores the practical work behind a conference, from logistics to collaboration with event organisers and the contribution of volunteers. Professor Ramnarayan highlights the teamwork involved in creating a meeting for the paediatric critical-care community and the value of providing opportunities for colleagues to learn, exchange ideas and connect.

This interview took place at the PCCS Annual Scientific Meeting 2026 in London.

This PCCS conference, I am the chair of the organising committee and there are three of us on the organising committee, myself and two colleagues from PICU at St Mary's. We've been working on this conference preparation for about a nearly a year and a half, probably closer to 15 months, and it's been very steady progress. Main tasks at the beginning were identifying the venue and choosing the right place.

We saw a few places. We wanted a northwest London vibe, so we stuck to northwest London. We saw Wembley, Chelsea football stadium, here at Lord's, and we found this was the best venue for the size of the hall, the number of participants we could have here, but also how beautiful it was. And we chose here.

And then I think over the last year the main focus has been developing the scientific programme, getting the speakers, and we wanted to make sure our speakers came from a variety of backgrounds: adult intensive care, trauma, clinical trials and research. And we just wanted to make sure that we showcase the breadth of expertise that there is at Imperial College at St Mary's across West London. And that was our goal really, to try and get a great programme together because we wanted to reflect on the broad strengths of PCCS but also the broad strengths of our Northwest London collaboration.

And then as it got closer to the conference, it was more about the logistics, trying to make sure the speakers knew where they were going, all the organising committee members were all together and on the same page about priorities, how to make the speaker presentations work. And we had the help of Simon Hubbard from Unified Events to help us with a lot of the abstract management, the presentations, liaising with the AV team, liaising with the venue, organising the sponsors and organising the charity partner linkages etc.

So yes, it's been a team effort, a massive team effort, and we had eight volunteers for this conference who all put their time aside freely and have contributed to making sure the whole thing runs smoothly. So massive team
