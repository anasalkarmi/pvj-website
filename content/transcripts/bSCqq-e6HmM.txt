KEY POINTS
- The UK paediatric critical care community has built an evidence base of randomised clinical trials to identify the best treatments for critically ill children.
- Collegiality, collaboration and links with methodologists for data methods and modelling underpin this work.
- Complete data collection and a high degree of adherence to the interventions make the trials eligible for high-impact peer-reviewed publication.
TRANSCRIPT
In my plenary presentation, I was setting out the amazing achievements of the paediatric critical care community in delivering an evidence base in the form of randomised clinical trials to identify the best treatments for critically ill children. They've had a phenomenal output, and I set out the very many factors that I thought were responsible for that.

Those factors were the general collegiality and collaborative spirit of the paediatric critical care community, and the investment they've made in linking up with methodologists for data methods and modelling to underpin the important work they do. I think the community itself delivering the trials on the ground. They've all been published in very high ranked peer-reviewed scientific publications, but that's because they're so well delivered on the ground. And delivery often is the completeness of the information that's collected in the trial, but also the adherence to the intervention.

And it's really important that when someone's randomised to the intervention being evaluated that that intervention is actually delivered as specified. And it's phenomenal that there's such a high degree of adherence in paediatric critical care trials that makes them then eligible, once analysed and written up, to be published in these, as I say, very high impact peer-reviewed scientific publications. Which means that the information that the paediatric critical care society is generating in the UK gets celebrated and communicated worldwide.
