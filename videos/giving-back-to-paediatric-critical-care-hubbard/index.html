Giving back to paediatric critical care

  • PCCS Conference 2026
  • Lord's, London
  • Published 7 November 2026
  • 4:03 min
  • Simon HubbardDirector of Unified Events

Key points

  • The speaker's family experienced paediatric intensive care first-hand when their 11-month old daughter fell ill with an extremely rare genetic condition.
  • Conversations with a member of the PICU team led to helping organise the critical care nephrology conference in 2023, and then to specialising in this work.
  • Supporting conference education is a way of giving back to the doctors and nurses who supported the family and of keeping their daughter's memory alive.

Simon Hubbard, Director of Unified Events, reflects on the personal motivation behind his work supporting paediatric critical-care conferences and education. He describes his family’s experience of paediatric intensive care and how it has shaped his commitment to the critical-care community. The interview connects the practical work of organising events with the opportunities they create for professionals to learn, share knowledge and build relationships. Simon discusses why supporting these meetings matters to him and how conference education offers a way to contribute to the care of children and families.

This interview took place at the PCCS Annual Scientific Meeting 2026 in London.

How I got involved with PCCS and paediatric intensive care, it's sort of a personal story, a bit of fate involved as well.

Because of my family we have firsthand experience of what an amazing sector this is and the incredible work all the clinicians do in supporting children, families that are affected, whether it's illness, injury and the impact that has on lives. My family's lives changed overnight on the 23rd of February 2019 when our 11-month old daughter fell ill with an extremely rare genetic condition that we had no awareness of at the time.

She caught the flu but unfortunately because of the genetic condition she had, which is called [unclear], her antibodies attacked her brain rather than the flu virus, causing her to have a seizure and a bleed on the brain which caused irreversible brain damage. We ended up spending three and a half months in King's College [unclear] units, and then a further three months at Brighton HDU.

It was while we were in King's that we met a number of the amazing staff and team that supported us. And one of those being Professor Akash Deep, and it was my conversations with Akash and then him asking me if I could support him with a conference that he was organizing, even though he said it wasn't probably the right timing, but when the time was right would I be able to help. So I helped him organise the critical care nephrology conference in 2023, which at the time was the largest conference globally. We made the national press for helping train doctors, nurses from around the world around kidney failing children and putting children on dialysis safely.

And then from there, we sort of specialised in trying to, as a family, give back to all of the amazing doctors, nurses that have supported us and helped us through the challenges that we had with our daughter. So it's sort of a bit of a debt of honour, I guess, in terms of our work in this industry sector.

Unfortunately, our daughter when she was 3 and a half caught COVID and wasn't strong enough to battle it. So for us as a family, it's a very personal thing in terms of keeping her memory alive, being able to support the education of medical advancements, certainly in terms of the paediatric sector, and hopefully more awareness of [unclear] condition as well, and so that hopefully it helps save even more children's lives.

And it's amazing that we're here today involved. The event has been incredible. Really, really proud of everyone that's been involved. It's been a lot of hard work.
