KEY POINTS
- Nine children with status epilepticus could not be extubated locally because of a lack of knowledge of the guidelines.
- After teaching and discussion of the Children's Acute Transport Service guidelines, two children were extubated locally.
- Local extubation avoids unnecessary transport to paediatric intensive care and the costs involved, and can be adopted by other trusts.
TRANSCRIPT
So today we presented a poster about local extubation in status epilepticus in children. We conducted a study at a busy district general hospital in London looking at children who have come in with prolonged seizures and children that need to be intubated and whether we could extubate children locally at our trust. So we conducted this prospectively and retrospectively following introduction of guidelines.

After we implemented new guidelines and had a talk about extubation using the Children's Acute Transport Service guidelines, we were able to extubate children locally. So initially we had nine children who came in with status epilepticus who were unable to extubate because of lack of knowledge, no idea about the guidelines etc.

Following implementation of new discussion about the guidelines, teaching about the guidelines, we then were able to extubate two children locally, therefore avoiding unnecessary transport to paediatric intensive care and the costs involved with that and precious PICU beds in London. So this is something that can be adopted by a lot of different trusts just following the simple guideline to allow them to extubate children locally who are coming in with status epilepticus.
