Connecting the PCCS community through communication

  • PCCS Conference 2026
  • Lord's, London
  • Published 7 November 2026

Key points

  • PCCS communications are becoming more responsive and more professional, with an interest in advocacy for the society.
  • The communications lead wants to hear from members.
  • Members can email the PCCS email address with anything they want advertised amongst the 1400 members.

Arun Ghose, PCCS Communications Lead and Intensive Care Consultant at Birmingham Children’s Hospital, discusses communication and advocacy across the paediatric critical-care community. He reflects on making professional communications more responsive and useful, and creating opportunities for members to share their work. The interview explores how PCCS channels can help connect colleagues, highlight news and opportunities, and support the wider interests of the specialty. Arun encourages members to contribute and take part in building a more connected community through shared information and professional engagement.

This interview took place at the PCCS Annual Scientific Meeting 2026 in London.

Hello. My name is Arun Ghose. I'm one of the intensive care consultants at Birmingham Children's Hospital, but I'm here on PCCS Council business. I'm the communications lead for the Paediatric Critical Care Society.

Now, what I really wanted to tell you about is how we are changing communications in PCCS. We're becoming more responsive. We're professionalising what we do and we're interested in advocacy for our society. We're also interested in hearing from you as members.

And what I'd like you to do is email us on the PCCS email, and if you email us with anything you want advertised amongst the 1400 members, I can share that with you and with them. So please keep in touch and anything you want shared, send to me. Thank you.
