KEY POINTS
- Taking part in public and patient engagement research gives a parent the chance to give something back after a child's PICU stay.
- The research team was flexible and adaptable about meeting times and length, which helped a parent with a young child feel part of the project.
- Parent contributors can reflect on the processes, how methods should be used, which questions families feel comfortable with, and how projects should be tailored.
TRANSCRIPT
Hello, I'm Amy Dent and I'm a parent, and my little boy was in PICU in December 2025. I'm really grateful for the opportunity to be part of the public patient engagement research, because it's provided a great opportunity for me to give something back. I felt supported throughout the project.

My little boy was 6 months when he was in PICU. So he's now 18 months. They're still at their younger ages and they've been so flexible in making me feel part of the project and welcome. They've been adaptable in relation to timings for meetings and how long you can stay in a meeting. Even if my little boy has had to be in the background of some of them, it's been a great opportunity.

I've been able to reflect on the processes that they go through, maybe how the methods should be used, what questions we feel comfortable with, and how they should tailor their projects going forward. So it's been great to be part of it. Thank you so much.
