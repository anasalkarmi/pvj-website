KEY POINTS
- After the GIRFT recommendation for care closer to home, ambulance fleet mileage reduced by around 4,000 miles and diesel consumption by 400 litres.
- In a sample week, the standard journey for families fell from about 56 miles in 2022 to about 26 miles in 2025.
- The study could not speak to patients qualitatively, so improved patient experience was inferred from the reduced mileage.
TRANSCRIPT
My research was to do a review of the transport service, one of the London transport services, to look at if the change in the GIRFT getting it right first time recommendation for care to be closer to home, and using operational delivery networks for PICU where there is a hub and spoke model, which translates into that children would be cared in the PICU at the nearest hub to them. And previous to that children were being travelled quite far distances when they needed a PICU admission.

So post GIRFT, which recommendation came out in 2020 2022, 95% of children was the target that they'd be cared for in the PICU closest to their home. We wanted to see what the impact was that on a transport service in terms of had it reduced our mileage, and for patient experience whether it meant that patients are actually travelling lower distances to get to their children who were admitted.

And when we looked at the years before the GIRFT recommendation in 2022 and then years after the GIRFT recommendation, we found that indeed mileage for our ambulance fleet had reduced by around 4,000 miles. Diesel consumption had also reduced by 400 litres of diesel and that's associated with a reduced carbon dioxide emissions, as well as the associated air pollution of burning diesel fuel.

And when we looked at a sample week for these families in 2022 versus 2025, we found that they had approximately half the distance travelled. So in 2022 about 56 miles on a standard journey compared to in 2025 more like 26 miles, and that was associated again with reduced travel time, reduced mileage and reduced carbon dioxide emissions. So overall aligning with NHS priorities of better value, sustainability, better patient care, better patient experience.

What we couldn't do was qualitatively talk to the patients and find out the impact on them directly, but we could look at the reduced mileage and infer from that improved patient experience.
