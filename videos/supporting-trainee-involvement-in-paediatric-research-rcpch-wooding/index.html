Supporting trainee involvement in paediatric research: RCPCH Trainee Research Network

Key points

  • The Trainee Research Network supports trainees, resident doctors and the wider paediatric workforce to get more involved in research.
  • Local trainee reps help members find research in their region, and national study days run twice a year, one entry level and one more advanced.
  • A monthly online research seminar called TREES runs on the last Tuesday of every month.

Dr Eva Wooding, co-chair of the RCPCH Trainee Research Network (TRN) and academic clinical fellow in paediatrics, discusses the network’s mission to support trainees, resident doctors, and the wider paediatric workforce in becoming more involved with research. She highlights that research is essential for advancing child health and is valued by young people themselves. The college supports research engagement through local trainee representatives who help members discover research opportunities in their regions, as well as through national initiatives such as biannual study days—one entry-level and one advanced—open to all resident doctors, trust doctors, and the broader child health workforce. These events provide practical skills, boost CVs, and help participants understand the relevance of research to clinical practice. Additionally, the TRN offers a monthly online research seminar, ‘TREES’, on the last Tuesday of each month, which covers a wide range of topics and supports research competencies and portfolio development. Dr Wooding encourages anyone interested to explore the TRN section on the RCPCH website for more information and to reach out to the network with suggestions for future events. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.
