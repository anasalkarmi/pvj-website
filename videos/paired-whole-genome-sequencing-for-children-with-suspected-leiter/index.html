Paired whole genome sequencing for children with suspected cancer

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 22 June 2025
  • 1:13 min
  • Dr Sarah LeiterCambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust

Key points

  • Whole genome sequencing was implemented by the NHS in England in 2021 for children with suspected cancer.
  • In a study of 281 children, about 7% had a change in management attributable purely to whole genome sequencing.
  • Paediatrics works with an underserved community in research, and getting involved, finding mentorship and following opportunities is encouraged.

Dr Sarah Leiter, Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, provides insights into the implementation of paired whole genome sequencing (WGS) for children with suspected cancer. The discussion highlights how routine WGS can be delivered as part of clinical care, offering significant benefits by rapidly identifying genetic changes that inform diagnosis and treatment decisions. Dr Leiter emphasises the importance of this approach in improving outcomes for children with suspected cancer, enabling more precise and individualized management strategies. She outlines how integrating WGS into routine clinical pathways can change clinical management and support families facing complex diagnostic journeys. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.

So our research work was looking at, so whole genome sequencing was implemented for children with suspected cancers by the NHS in England in 2021 and we set out to figure out whether or not this provides any benefit to the children if you sequence everybody who walks in through the front door of suspected cancer. And what we showed during our work, looking at a total of 281 children, is that in about 7% of children a change in management can be purely attributed to the fact that you've done whole genome sequencing.

I think research is really important for paediatrics. We are often working with an underserved community in terms of research and so I would encourage anybody to get involved with research and it's really about looking around these opportunities around you and looking out for mentorship and just getting involved really and seeing where the journey takes you.
