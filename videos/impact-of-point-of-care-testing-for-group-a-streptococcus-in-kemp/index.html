Impact of point-of-care testing for Group A Streptococcus in the paediatric emergency department

Key points

  • An audit of 82 randomly selected children compared those swabbed with point of care testing for group A strep and those not swabbed.
  • Children who were not swabbed spent an average of 317 minutes in the department, compared with 186 minutes for those who tested positive.
  • Swabbed children had lower antibiotic prescription rates, fewer admissions and readmissions, and less clinician bias.

Dr Alison Kemp and Dr Connie Foxhall, Warrington Hospital, provide insights into a retrospective audit evaluating the implementation of point-of-care testing for Group A Streptococcus in the paediatric emergency department. The discussion highlights the analysis of 82 children randomly selected between November 2023 and February 2024, focusing on antibiotic prescription rates, reattendance, and length of stay. The findings showed that children who received point-of-care testing had a significantly shorter average wait time (186 minutes) compared to those who did not (317 minutes). Antibiotic prescription rates were lower among tested children, supporting improved antimicrobial stewardship and reduced clinician bias in decision-making. Drs Kemp and Foxhall emphasise that point-of-care testing enhances patient care by lowering wait times, reducing unnecessary admissions and readmissions, and supporting timely, evidence-based treatment. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.

Hi, my name is Dr Alison Kemp. I'm a specialty paediatric trainee. Hi, my name is Dr Connie Foxhall. I'm a second year foundation doctor at Warrington.

Myself and Alison conducted a retrospective audit on the implementation of point of care testing for group A strep at Warrington General Hospital in the paediatric emergency department between November 2023 and February 2024. We did a statistical analysis of 82 children that were randomly selected and our main aim was to assess antibiotic prescriptions, reattendance rates and also the length of stay in the department.

So from this audit we found that of the children that tested positive we had an average wait time of 186 minutes in the department, which is really close to the 4 hour ED time that we sort of aim to work towards. And we noticed that those children that didn't have swabs also actually spent 317 minutes on average in the department. So that significantly shows there's a difference between those that are swabbed and those that are not swabbed.

And we found that in the children that were swabbed, the antibiotic prescription rates were lower. We also found that this then supports point of care testing in lowering wait times in the emergency department. We also found that this enhances patient care because we had a lower admission rate and readmissions to ED. And we found that it reduced the antibiotic prescriptions in general and made less clinician bias when looking at these children using the [unclear] criteria. So in conclusion, we found that point of care testing does enhance patient care and antimicrobial stewardship within the ED.
