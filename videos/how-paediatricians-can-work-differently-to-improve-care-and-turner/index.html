How paediatricians can work differently to improve care and wellbeing

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 4 July 2025
  • 2:21 min
  • Professor Steve TurnerPresident of the Royal College of Paediatrics and Child Health (RCPCH)

Key points

  • Caring for patients and their families needs to stay at the heart of paediatric practice.
  • Rapid review clinics, and involving senior decision makers early, reduce tests and treatment trials that are not needed.
  • Hospital at home and open-ended discharge plans can reduce hospital care and unnecessary GP or emergency department visits after discharge.

Professor Steve Turner shares concise insights on how paediatricians can work differently to enhance patient care and improve their own professional wellbeing. He emphasises that caring for patients and their families must remain central to paediatric practice. Key strategies he highlights include:

- Rapid review clinics to see children promptly during business hours, avoiding unnecessary overnight visits to tired paediatricians.

- Early involvement of senior decision-makers (ST4 level and above, including consultants) to reduce unnecessary tests and ineffective treatments, ensuring the right care is delivered at the right time and place.

- Hospital at home models, enabling more children to receive care in their own homes rather than in hospital, which requires a cultural shift for clinicians accustomed to hospital-based care.

- Improved discharge planning, providing support to families after hospital discharge to reduce unnecessary GP or emergency department visits. He advocates for open-ended discharge plans where parents can easily re-engage with paediatric services if needed.

Professor Turner’s reflections offer practical, patient-centered approaches to transform paediatric care delivery and support clinicians in their roles. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.

So, if I was asked the question, as I often am, how should paediatricians look to work differently? I would have a lot to say, but I've only got two minutes, so I'll keep it short and sweet. I think the first thing we need to remember as paediatricians is that we're here to care and look after our patients and also their families. And that needs to be at the heart of what we do. But there are a lot of things that we can do differently that can improve the care that we provide for our patients, and also might also make us feel a little bit more comfortable at work.

So in no particular order, we know that rapid review clinics are a very effective way of seeing children in a timely manner that doesn't involve people travelling in the middle of the night to see some poor tired paediatrician who's been up all night. They can be seen within the business hours when there's more people around.

We know that the earlier a senior decision maker, that would be ST4 and above including consultants, gets involved in a patient's journey, the less likely tests which aren't needed will be done, the less likely trials of treatment that aren't going to help are avoided, and the right care, the right place, the right time is delivered to the right child. So getting senior decision makers involved early makes a big difference.

Another thing that is happening around us, particularly in London, is hospital at home. We provide a lot of care for children in the hospital and a lot of that can be provided in home, and that's going to take quite a leap of faith from people like me who've only provided care for children in the hospital context. So hospital at home is a third way.

And the fourth thing, I could go on but I'm going to keep this the last one, is our discharge planning. When we're discharging a child from the hospital, we need to give them support. We know that 10% of children discharged from hospital will go and see the GP or emergency department in the following week. They shouldn't be doing that. It's the same thing. They should come back to paediatric services and from clinic. I think we can trust parents to say, "I think your child's symptoms are controlled. I think you're looking after them really well. I'm not going to arrange to see you again, but here's my secretary's number. I'm giving you an open-ended discharge. We won't plan to meet again.
