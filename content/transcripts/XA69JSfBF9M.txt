KEY POINTS
- FibroScan is a non-invasive marker of steatosis and fibrosis in children, and its results were correlated with blood tests, BMI and ultrasound findings in 76 children.
- There was a significant correlation between BMI and both fibrosis and steatosis.
- MASLD is for the most part a reversible disease, so identification is a good time to intervene.
TRANSCRIPT
So our study, our poster, is about metabolically associated liver disease in children. We helped in a clinic where we perform FibroScans, and a FibroScan is a non-invasive marker of steatosis and fibrosis in children, and it looks at their liver and also their spleen size. We correlated the results of the FibroScan with other biomarkers such as blood tests, BMI and ultrasound findings.

We looked at 76 children and we found a significant correlation between BMI and fibrosis and steatosis, and found a correlation between ultrasound findings of steatosis on the liver and also fibrosis scores and steatosis scores on their FibroScan. We found that a high FibroScan correlated with higher ALT markers and ultrasound findings. We identified that this would be a perfect time to intervene, because for the most part MASLD is a reversible disease, but can have a high disease burden if not treated appropriately at the right time.
