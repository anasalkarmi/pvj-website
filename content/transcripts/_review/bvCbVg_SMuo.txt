KEY POINTS
- One liquid medicine was found to come in 14 different strengths, which makes it easy for parents and carers to get doses wrong.
- For virtually all medicines, one or maybe two concentrations would do for everybody from neonates to adults.
- A formulary of recommended concentrations is being created in the hope that those strengths will go through to licence.
TRANSCRIPT
Whilst we've seen a big move to using tablets in children as much as possible for all sorts of good reasons, we still use loads and loads of liquids. And one of the biggest problems that we have is either at the point of outpatients or discharge or in the community, parents tend to remember the number of millilitres they were given, are supposed to give.

Liquids come as all sorts of concentrations. In fact, we've just done a bit of a study and there's one liquid which comes in 14 different strengths. And we wonder why parents and carers get it wrong. We've done some other work to show that for virtually all medicines, you can get away with either one or maybe two for the odds liquid for everybody, all the way down from neonates all the way up to adults. And therefore, we should try and stick to one concentration. It avoids that problem with millilitres and parents remembering the number of millilitres but picking up different strengths of liquids from different community pharmacies, from pharmacy in the hospital or wherever they get them from.

So it's time to move on to standardising and what we've tried to do is starting to create a formulary of the concentration that we think is the right one to use. The ideal of that is that also we hope that those strengths will go through to licence. At the moment, industry doesn't know what to create because we're all using so many different strengths. But in fact, by moving on certainly our unlicensed medicines to one strength, we get to a point where hopefully they go to licence.

And if we all use only one strength of licensed, we hope that there aren't quite as many licensed different strengths being made. For most there aren't, but for some even the licensed, they're making four or five strengths. It doesn't help with safety and it's time we moved on.
