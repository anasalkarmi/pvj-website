Five stories this week

The strongest items from both lists, each in about seventy words.

JAMA · 5 Nov 2026

Newborn vitamin K refusals rise as US infant bleeding diagnoses climb

Two US studies published on 5 November, just before this week, drew the highest Altmetric scores in this roundup. In more than 5.2 million infants, those who did not receive intramuscular vitamin K had a higher adjusted risk of bleeding by six months (adjusted risk ratio 1.47), and a higher risk of intracranial bleeding. The design is observational, but the findings bear directly on how teams counsel families who decline prophylaxis.

USA (hospitals in 50 states; Children's Hospital of Philadelphia)Read the paper

The Lancet · 12 Nov 2026

Empirical TB treatment did not save more infants with HIV and severe pneumonia

In the EMPIRICAL trial across 19 hospitals in six African countries, 563 infants with HIV and severe pneumonia were randomised to empirical tuberculosis treatment or a clinically and microbiologically guided approach. Among 558 analysed, twelve-month mortality was 48% with empirical treatment and 43% without (adjusted HR 1.12, 95% CI 0.87–1.43): no benefit was shown. Nearly half of the comparison group started TB treatment anyway, and mortality in both arms remains high.

Côte d'Ivoire, Malawi, Mozambique, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe (19 hospitals)Read the paper

The Lancet · 10 Nov 2026

A US$6 parenting app reduced sexual violence against adolescent girls in Tanzania

A cluster-randomised trial in 80 Tanzanian sub-wards tested ParentApp, an offline-first digital parenting programme delivered on low-cost phones with one in-person session and WhatsApp support. Among 2,396 adolescents, girls in intervention clusters reported less past-month sexual violence (IRR 0.43, 95% CI 0.21–0.86); no effect was shown for boys. Past-month maltreatment was also lower, at a delivery cost of about US$6 per family at scale.

Tanzania (80 sub-wards)Read the paper

JAMA Pediatrics · 13 Nov 2026

Extra time and standing desks did not help children with ADHD in a randomised trial

A crossover randomised trial in a US summer treatment programme tested classroom accommodations for children with ADHD in kindergarten to grade 6, alongside methylphenidate and behaviour therapy. Extended time, frequent breaks, low-distraction workspaces and standing desks brought no significant benefit for behaviour or academic outcomes. Medication improved work output and quiz scores, and both medication and behaviour therapy reduced rule violations; figures to be checked against the full text. School-based replication is needed.

USARead the paper

Pediatrics · 10 Nov 2026

Habitus-corrected length tapes outperform Broselow for resuscitation weight estimates

A systematic review and meta-analysis of 250 studies, nine in children actually needing resuscitation, compared tools for estimating weight and calculating drug doses. The PAWPER tape, a length-based tape with body-habitus correction, outperformed the Broselow tape for time to drug administration, dose accuracy and weight accuracy (odds ratio 2.48 for estimates within 10%). Age-based formulas were consistently inaccurate. No study reported patient outcomes.

Canada (lead authors); international literatureRead the paper

    Nonreceipt of Newborn Vitamin K and Risk of Bleeding

    JAMA · 5 Nov 2026 Published before this week

    570
    Findings and why it is being discussed
    Study
    Retrospective cohort, Epic Cosmos dataset, 5,240,780 infants born at 35-43 weeks, 2017-2025. USA (hospitals in 50 states; Children's Hospital of Philadelphia).
    Key findings
    Non-receipt of newborn intramuscular vitamin K rose from 3.1% in 2017 to 6.8% in 2025. Infants who did not receive it had a higher risk of bleeding by 6 months (288.0 vs 185.9 per 10,000; adjusted RR 1.47, 95% CI 1.43-1.51), of intracranial bleeding (33.0 vs 13.2 per 10,000; adjusted RR 2.43, 95% CI 2.26-2.62) and of death with concurrent bleeding during the birth admission (2.9 vs 1.1 per 10,000; adjusted RR 2.69). Observational design based on diagnosis codes.
    Why the attention
    Reported together with rank 1 in the same Reuters story and by Contemporary Pediatrics (Contemporary Pediatrics).
    Measured attention
    570 (64 news outlets, 1 blog, 68 X users, 3 Facebook pages) (Altmetric)

Most clinically important

Ten papers published this week, ranked by PVJ's editorial judgement of the strength of the evidence and its potential to change practice. This list is a judgement, not a measurement.

  1. 1

    Empirical tuberculosis treatment in infants living with HIV hospitalised with severe pneumonia in Africa (EMPIRICAL): a multicentre, randomised, open-label, 2 × 2 factorial, controlled superiority trial

    The Lancet · 12 Nov 2026 (DOI registered; issue dated 14 Nov)

    Why it matters

    Does not support blanket empirical TB treatment for infants with HIV and severe pneumonia over a clinically and microbiologically guided approach; mortality of 43-48% shows how much this group still needs.

    Evidence and limitations
    Study
    Multicentre open-label 2 × 2 factorial superiority RCT; 563 infants aged 28-365 days enrolled, 558 analysed. Côte d'Ivoire, Malawi, Mozambique, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe (19 hospitals).
    Key findings
    Death by 12 months was 48% (132/276) with empirical TB treatment and 43% (121/282) without (adjusted HR 1.12, 95% CI 0.87-1.43; p=0.39); serious adverse events did not differ. In the comparison groups, 46% started TB treatment anyway, at a median of 4 days.
    Limitations
    Open label; heavy crossover in the comparison arm narrowed the contrast; a non-significant result with a wide CI is not proof of equivalence; the valganciclovir comparison was reported separately.
  2. 2

    A blended digital parenting intervention for the prevention of sexual violence, exploitative sex, and maltreatment of children at scale in Tanzania: a cluster-randomised, controlled trial

    The Lancet · 10 Nov 2026

    Why it matters

    A low-cost, scalable violence-prevention programme built into WHO-UNICEF's Parenting for Lifelong Health, directly relevant to child-protection services and policy in low-resource settings.

    Evidence and limitations
    Study
    Cluster-randomised controlled trial (ParentApp vs a hygiene app), 2,396 adolescents aged 10-17 and their caregivers, 18-month follow-up. Tanzania (80 sub-wards).
    Key findings
    Girls in ParentApp clusters reported less past-month sexual violence victimisation (IRR 0.43, 95% CI 0.21-0.86); with no effect for boys (IRR 1.21, 0.59-2.43). Past-month maltreatment was lower in ParentApp clusters (adolescent report IRR 0.92, 0.88-0.96), at a delivery cost of US$6 per family at scale.
    Limitations
    Self-reported outcomes; participants and researchers unmasked; no shown benefit for boys on sexual violence; an Oxford web page quotes a different figure for a different outcome and time point.
  3. 3

    Weight Estimation and Dose Calculation in Resuscitation: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

    Pediatrics · 10 Nov 2026

    Why it matters

    Informs which tool emergency, resuscitation and transport teams stock and teach, and argues against age-based formulas.

    Evidence and limitations
    Study
    Systematic review and meta-analysis, 250 studies, 9 in children actually requiring resuscitation. Canada (lead authors); international literature.
    Key findings
    The PAWPER tape (length-based with body-habitus correction) beat the Broselow tape for time to drug administration, dose accuracy and weight-estimation accuracy in all analyses (OR 2.48, 95% CI 2.04-3.02 for estimates within 10%). Age-based formulas were consistently inaccurate, and results held across country income levels.
    Limitations
    No study reported patient outcomes; few studies in real resuscitations; children longer than the tape or with skeletal deformity or growth restriction were excluded from many source studies.
  4. 4

    Procalcitonin- and Presepsin-Guided Antibiotics for Early-Onset Sepsis in Preterm Infants: A Randomized Clinical Trial

    JAMA Network Open · 12 Nov 2026

    Why it matters

    Biomarker guidance cut antibiotic exposure sharply, but this trial does not support adopting it; it shapes neonatal stewardship debate and the design of the next trial.

    Evidence and limitations
    Study
    Open-label multicentre non-inferiority RCT, 283 very preterm infants. Italy (17 NICUs).
    Key findings
    Survival without major morbidity was 53% with biomarker-guided antibiotics and 55% with universal treatment (risk difference -1.8 percentage points, 90% CI -11.9 to 8.3), so non-inferiority was not shown. Antibiotics were given to 58% vs 98% of infants, and no infant developed early-onset sepsis.
    Limitations
    Probably underpowered (event-free survival well below the 72% assumed); infants with major sepsis risk factors excluded; open label with blinded assessors; a per-protocol estimate may contain a typo (not verified).
  5. 5

    Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder Accommodations, Behavior Therapy, and Stimulant Medication: A Randomized Clinical Trial

    JAMA Pediatrics · 13 Nov 2026

    Why it matters

    Questions reliance on classroom accommodations as stand-alone ADHD support, which matters for paediatricians, psychologists, school nurses and education plans.

    Evidence and limitations
    Study
    Crossover RCT in summer treatment programme classrooms, children in kindergarten to grade 6 with ADHD; 280 enrolled, 235 completed. USA.
    Key findings
    Extended time, frequent breaks, low-distraction workspaces and standing desks, alone or combined, brought no significant benefit for behaviour or academic outcomes. Methylphenidate improved seatwork productivity and quiz scores, and both medication and behaviour therapy reduced rule violations; the abstract and results text assign these effects differently, so figures to be checked against the full text.
    Limitations
    Summer-programme setting and short duration; needs replication in ordinary schools; accommodations used variably; internal inconsistencies in the published abstract.
  6. 6

    Civil Society Organization-Led Home Visit Intervention to Reduce Unintentional Child Injury in Rural China: A Cluster Randomized Clinical Trial

    JAMA Network Open · 11 Nov 2026

    Why it matters

    A home-visiting model delivered by civil-society organisations rather than health services, which could extend injury prevention to rural communities.

    Evidence and limitations
    Study
    Cluster RCT with blinded assessors, 24 communities, 360 households, children aged 3-12. China (Guizhou and Shaanxi).
    Key findings
    Four-month injury incidence was 11.8% in intervention households and 19.1% in controls (adjusted OR 0.36, 95% CI 0.18-0.73, intention to treat with multiple imputation). Caregiver supervision scores rose and household hazard scores fell.
    Limitations
    Injuries reported by unblinded caregivers; only 4 months of follow-up; covariate imbalance; some registered child-level secondary outcomes not collected.
  7. 7

    Comparative efficacy and tolerability of pharmacological interventions for the acute treatment of obsessive-compulsive disorder across the lifespan: a systematic review and network meta-analysis

    The Lancet Psychiatry · 11 Nov 2026

    Why it matters

    Comparative evidence for shared decisions on OCD medication in children and adolescents, weighing efficacy against tolerability.

    Evidence and limitations
    Study
    Systematic review and network meta-analysis; 9 paediatric trials with 822 children and adolescents analysed separately. International (52 RCTs; lead authors in Amsterdam).
    Key findings
    In young people, clomipramine (SMD -1.19, 95% CI -1.73 to -0.64), sertraline, fluoxetine and fluvoxamine were all more efficacious than placebo (low certainty), and clomipramine outperformed individual SSRIs (very low certainty). Only sertraline was less tolerable than placebo in young people (OR 4.51, 95% CI 1.36-14.96).
    Limitations
    Small paediatric evidence base; low to very low certainty for paediatric comparisons; short-term (8-16 week) outcomes only.
  8. 8

    ERN-RITA/PReS recommendations for the diagnosis, classification, monitoring, and treatment of childhood idiopathic (primary) angiitis of the central nervous system

    Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases · 12 Nov 2026

    Why it matters

    The first international guidance for a rare, serious childhood disease; shared definitions make registries and trials possible across rheumatology, neurology, radiology and genetics.

    Evidence and limitations
    Study
    Recommendations: systematic review of 182 studies, modified Delphi survey and consensus conference. International panel of 42 experts from 15 countries (lead author in Hungary).
    Key findings
    The panel agreed 83 statements across seven clinical domains, including an "idiopathic/disease-associated" nomenclature, a paediatric vessel-wall imaging principle, a risk-stratification model, genome-wide sequencing as the preferred genetic test, and formal definitions of remission, relapse and refractory disease. No level 1 evidence existed for any domain.
    Limitations
    Consensus-based with no level 1 evidence; European-network led; effect on outcomes untested.
  9. 9

    Incidence and predictors of suboptimal functional outcomes despite successful reperfusion in paediatric stroke: a cohort study from the Save ChildS Pro registry

    The Lancet Child & Adolescent Health · 10 Nov 2026

    Why it matters

    First description of "futile recanalisation" in children; cautions against importing adult prognostic tools and supports aiming for the best possible reperfusion.

    Evidence and limitations
    Study
    Retrospective analysis of prospectively collected registry data; 101 children aged 28 days to 18 years with successful thrombectomy recanalisation. International (45 centres in 12 countries).
    Key findings
    18% of children had a suboptimal 90-day outcome (mRS >2), against 40-60% reported in adults. Lower final reperfusion grade (adjusted OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.23-0.84) and younger age (adjusted OR 0.87 per year) were associated with suboptimal outcome; baseline PedNIHSS and ASPECTS were not.
    Limitations
    Small event count and exploratory; retrospective; needs external validation; 90-day outcomes only.
  10. 10

    Sepsis Point Prevalence Across Ibero-Latin America PICUs: The 2024 Sepsis Prevalence Assessment Database in Pediatrics (SPREAD PED IBERO-LATAM)

    Pediatric Critical Care Medicine · 13 Nov 2026

    Why it matters

    Large, LMIC-inclusive data linking sepsis outcomes to resources and vaccination, relevant to sepsis criteria, vaccination policy and PICU planning.

    Evidence and limitations
    Study
    Prospective multinational point-prevalence study. Ibero-Latin America: 168 PICUs in 21 countries (Latin America, Spain, Portugal).
    Key findings
    Of 1,318 children screened, 340 met criteria for clinical sepsis (point prevalence 25.8%); PICU mortality was 13.0% and in-hospital mortality 15.1%. Renal and immunological dysfunction, abnormal nutritional status, lack of vaccination and admission to lower-resource units were associated with higher PICU mortality.
    Limitations
    One-day point-prevalence design; associations only; abstract seen in summary, so odds ratios and stated limitations not verified.

What was searched, how attention was measured, and what could not be checked.
