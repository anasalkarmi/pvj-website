KEY POINTS
- The unit was established in 1986 to study rare diseases, their incidence and prevalence, and outcomes for affected children.
- Its success led other countries to set up their own surveillance units, with Germany first in 1992, followed by Australia, New Zealand and Canada.
- These units continue to produce large numbers of studies and publications that improve understanding of rare diseases in children around the world.
TRANSCRIPT
So the British Paediatric Surveillance Unit was established in 1986 to study rare diseases, to understand their incidence, prevalence, and also potentially find out about the outcomes for these children who are affected with these rare diseases. It's been such a success over the last 30 or so years that other surveillance units have set up elsewhere around the world. The first to follow us was Germany in 1992. Subsequent to that, we've had units set up in Australia, New Zealand, Canada, and they continue to produce large numbers of studies and publications about rare disease, which is great for our understanding of what's happening for children with rare diseases around the world.
