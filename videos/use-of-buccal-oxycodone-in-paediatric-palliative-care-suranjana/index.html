Use of buccal oxycodone in paediatric palliative care

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 22 June 2025
  • 1:30 min
  • Dr S SuranjanaSpecialist in paediatric palliative care at Martin House Children’s Hospice

Key points

  • A small study of four patients gave the normal oxycodone oral solution by the buccal route instead of the enteral route.
  • Buccal administration of the oral solution worked just as well as giving it enterally via the NG tube or the PEG.
  • The children had neurological or genetic conditions, and the buccal route was used mainly for pain, including in one child at the end of life.

Dr S Suranjana, a specialist in paediatric palliative care at Martin House Children’s Hospice, provides insights into a research project focused on the use of buccal oxycodone for pain and agitation in children with life-limiting conditions. She highlights the innovative use of standard oral oxycodone solution administered via the buccal route, rather than through enteral tubes, and demonstrates its effectiveness in four patients with neurological and genetic conditions. Dr Suranjana emphasises the importance of flexible, patient-centred approaches in palliative care and outlines how this method could be considered for children who are unable to tolerate enteral administration or who require rapid symptom control. She expresses hope that the study will encourage further research into alternative routes of medication administration to improve comfort and outcomes for children and families. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.

We did this little research project when I was at Martin House Children's Hospice, which is based at Boston Spa. We are a big tertiary hospice and this was a small study consisting of four patients.

What we tried is using oxycodone in the buccal form. What was different about this is we used the normal oral solution, which is commonly given in the enteral form, to be used in the buccal form. And we found, it is a very small study consisting only of four patients, but we found that the buccal way of administering oxycodone using the oral solution works just as well as giving it in the enteral form via the NG tube or the PEG.

The four children consisted of two of them having a neurological condition and two of them having a genetic condition. One of the children also required oxycodone to be used in the end of life situation where they had pain and agitation.
