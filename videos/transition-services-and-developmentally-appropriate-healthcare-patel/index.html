Transition services and developmentally appropriate healthcare for young people

  • A survey of leads across the North Thames Paediatric Network assessed how transition and developmentally appropriate healthcare are delivered.
  • Only 54% of respondents regularly see young people alone, and 31% reported a gap in transition service provision.
  • Access to resources and support is inequitable, with an alarming gap in services affecting those aged 16 to 18.

Ria Patel, University College London, discusses her work assessing how transition and developmentally appropriate healthcare is being delivered accross the North Thames Paediatric Network in the UK. The team found that there is currently inequitable access to resources and support for healthcare professionals to provide transition and developmentally appropriate healthcare services accross the network with wide variation in service delivery. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2024 in Birmingham, UK.

So my name is Ria Patel, I'm a UCL medical student and I just completed an iBSc in paediatrics and child health. So the work that I presented during the RCPCH plenary abstract slot today was looking at transition and developmentally appropriate healthcare within the North Thames Paediatric Network. So we aimed to identify and assess how transition and developmentally appropriate healthcare are currently being delivered across the network in order to reduce inconsistencies and improve care for young people.

When I'm talking about transition, I'm referring to the planning and preparing for young people to move from paediatric to adult services. And developmentally appropriate healthcare is the requirement of catering to young people's stage of biopsychosocial development as opposed to just their chronological age. So these are the two concepts we were looking at. We disseminated an electronic survey to clinical and speciality leads across the network.

Some of our key findings are that only 54% of our respondents report regularly seeing young people alone and informing them of their right to confidentiality. Only 58% report adjusting the approach to care as young people develop, one of the key domains of developmentally appropriate healthcare. With regards to transition, around 76% of our respondents had access to a transition service. However, 31% indicated there was currently a gap in service provision, with those aged 16 to 18 being most commonly affected by this gap.

Overall what we found is there is currently inequitable access to resources and support for healthcare professionals to provide transition and developmentally appropriate healthcare to their patients. There's variation in how this care is currently being delivered and there is an alarming gap between services, particularly impacting those aged 16 to 18.

Some of the key take-home messages we shared at the RCPCH conference today were identifying a transition lead, engaging with trust management, seeing young people alone and adjusting the approach to care as they develop, and collaborating with adult services through a joint vision to improve [unclear] young people.
