AI-driven multimodal data processing for early detection of ADHD and depression in children

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 23 June 2025
  • 1:43 min
  • Dr Mohsen NaqviSenior Associate Professor (Reader) in Signal Processing and AI at Newcastle University

Key points

  • The research applies multimodal data processing and artificial intelligence to mental health detection, with ADHD and depression as the main tasks.
  • It uses video, audio, behaviour and questionnaire data, mirroring what clinicians observe when a patient comes to them.
  • The aim is an application that could indicate whether someone has ADHD or depression, developed in close collaboration with clinicians.

Dr Mohsen Naqvi, Senior Associate Professor (Reader) in Signal Processing and AI at Newcastle University, provides insights into interdisciplinary research at the intersection of engineering and medicine, focusing on the development of artificial intelligence tools for mental health detection in children. The discussion highlights the use of multimodal data—including video, audio, behavioural observations, and digital questionnaires—to create computer-based applications capable of identifying conditions such as ADHD and depression. Dr Naqvi explains how these tools aim to support clinicians by automating aspects of the diagnostic process, leveraging the widespread use of smart devices among children. He emphasises the importance of close collaboration between engineers and clinicians to ensure that AI solutions are both effective and clinically relevant, with the ultimate goal of developing accessible applications to help detect mental health issues early. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.
