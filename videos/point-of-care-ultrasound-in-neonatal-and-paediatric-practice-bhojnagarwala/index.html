Point-of-care ultrasound in neonatal and paediatric practice

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 23 June 2025
  • 2:24 min
  • Dr Bikash BhojnagarwalaConsultant neonatologist with expertise in paediatric cardiology at Medway NHS Foundation Trust

Key points

  • Point of care ultrasound is a vital tool for clinical decisions, but clinicians must understand their remit and use it judiciously.
  • It is a bedside assessment by the treating clinicians, unlike detailed echocardiography done by sonographers or radiologists.
  • The learning pathway starts with image acquisition, then interpretation, then applying it to supplement clinical decision making.

Dr Bikash Bhojnagarwala, consultant neonatologist with expertise in paediatric cardiology at Medway NHS Foundation Trust, discusses the evolving role of point-of-care ultrasound in neonatal and paediatric clinical settings. He highlights how point-of-care ultrasound has become a vital tool for making timely clinical decisions, especially for both neonatal and paediatric patients. Dr Bhojnagarwala emphasizes the importance of understanding the appropriate scope and limitations of this technology—encouraging clinicians to use it judiciously and in conjunction with other imaging modalities. He explains the distinction between bedside point-of-care ultrasound, which is performed by clinicians as part of their immediate patient assessment, and more detailed echocardiography or imaging performed by specialist sonographers or radiologists for objective reporting. Dr Bhojnagarwala outlines the learning pathway for clinicians, starting with image acquisition, progressing to interpretation, and ultimately integrating findings to supplement clinical decision-making. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.

It's fascinating, the changing landscape in the use of point of care ultrasound in clinical application for both neonatal patients as well as paediatric patients. What I think is that it is a vital tool to be used to make clinical decisions, but we need to understand our remit when the clinicians use the point of care, how to use it judiciously and how to use it in reference to the other modalities.

So when we talk about point of care ultrasound, it is a small part in this bigger spectrum of clinical imaging, which also includes targeted neonatal echocardiography, and also includes the wider group of congenital heart disease echocardiography, which is done mostly by the paediatric cardiologist. So here what we are doing in point of care ultrasound, what the clinicians are supposed to do, is to have a bedside assessment of the baby or the patient in conjunction with the clinical assessment of the patient and inform or [unclear] themselves of the decisions by that. That's the point of care ultrasound, and it differentiates from a detailed echocardiography or detailed assessment which is done by clinicians who are not looking after the baby. They have been asked, they are mostly sonographers or radiologists who come to do the scan, and their decisions are mostly objective based on what they are seeing, and then they give the report.

So this is where the difference is. So again, this is a very fluid part. You start with the point of care. You learn how to take image acquisitions and see how you interpret those image acquisitions, and then learn how to apply it and how to supplement your clinical decision making with this added tool.
