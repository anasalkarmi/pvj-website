  • Programme grants of 3 to 5 million pounds are meant to build capacity, sustainability and scalability in child health research.

Professor Paul Dimitri, Vice President for Science and Research at the Royal College of Paediatrics and Child Health (RCPCH), discusses the establishment of a national Child Health Research group focused on identifying and prioritising key areas in paediatric research. Through collaboration with key stakeholders, including RCPCH members and young people, the group identified priority areas such as safeguarding, obesity, mental health, neurodevelopmental paediatrics, and the integration of social care and public health. By presenting these priorities to the NIHR Strategy Board, dedicated funding and support for capacity building in child health research is prioritised. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, UK.

So what we did was we brought a group together. This was the child health research group, which formulated a document that was taken to the NIHR strategy board. That was back in 2022. We got unanimous support from all the members of the NIHR strategy board. That is one of the highest boards in the National Institute of Health and Care Research, led by the CEO Professor [unclear], and all the infrastructure leads and the programme leads as well as other allied organisations sit on that group.

What then happened was a series of meetings with the programme leads of some of the research programmes to try and identify funding for those priority areas of research. Where we hit upon best was actually our relationship with the programme grants group, the NIHR programme grants group, that worked with us collaboratively to set up programme development grants which will subsequently move on to programme grants to support funding for those areas of need. So the programme development grants launched in November 2024 and then two research calls came from that for NIHR programme development grants to support multi-sectoral, multi-professional research in those high impact areas.

So I'll go over those again: obesity, mental health, neurodevelopmental paediatrics, safeguarding, and also in areas of public health and social care. Those research rounds have passed now and what will subsequently happen is those applicants that were successful will be able to go on to apply for programme grants for applied research in 2026, 27, at which they'll be able to apply for between 3 and 5 million pounds for a programme grant to support their area of research in high impact areas. So those are areas where there's high prevalence of those particular problems.

The idea within those grants is that we also build in a capacity build into those grants so that we make sure that we build capacity into the system so that there are researchers that can take on those research in those areas for many years to come. So where we focused these was on capacity, sustainability and scalability. So where we could actually invest in the research, create a research workforce to focus on those areas and then scale that up really to a national level.
