KEY POINTS
- Up to a third of children with neuromuscular disorders have sleep disordered breathing.
- Sleep disordered breathing prognosticates survival and also supports quality of life, winter illness and time spent in hospital.
- Clinicians should keep a low threshold for suspecting it, and identify and treat it early for better outcomes.
