KEY POINTS
- The project merges meaningful co-production with young people and systems thinking to design out inequalities in the system.
- Young people in inner city Birmingham schools carried out community research on their health and the health of their communities.
- Their data was looked at through a human factors filter, and themes that emerged will be refined and fed back to improve local systems.
TRANSCRIPT
I'm delighted to be here in Glasgow talking about the project that we've done looking at how we merge the two disciplines of meaningful co-production, which is indicated by my pink glasses. Last year we had pink hats but this year we've got pink glasses to symbolise meaningful co-production with young people, trying to design out the inequalities of our system. So we've been doing a stream of work looking at that.

As part of that we've gone into schools within inner city Birmingham and asked young people around their health and the health of their communities. They've gone out and done some community research and with that data that they brought back, what we've done is looked at it with a human factors or ergonomic filter.

So as [unclear] was talking about this morning actually, we need to be thinking about systems when we're looking at improvement for our patients. And so what we tried to do is use the scientific background of systems thinking to look at the real time information that the young people in Birmingham were giving us.

And when we did that, what we realised is that there were themes that were emerging. And what we want to do is further refine the tool and to be able to pick out the themes to feed them back to improve the systems around these young people in the city they're living in.
