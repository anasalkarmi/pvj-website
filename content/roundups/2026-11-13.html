The Lancet · 12 Nov 2026

Empirical TB treatment did not save more infants with HIV and severe pneumonia

In the EMPIRICAL trial across 19 hospitals in six African countries, 563 infants with HIV and severe pneumonia were randomised to empirical tuberculosis treatment or a clinically and microbiologically guided approach. Among 558 analysed, twelve-month mortality was 48% with empirical treatment and 43% without (adjusted HR 1.12, 95% CI 0.87–1.43): no benefit was shown. Nearly half of the comparison group started TB treatment anyway, and mortality in both arms remains high.

A US$6 parenting app reduced sexual violence against adolescent girls in Tanzania

Pediatrics · 10 Nov 2026

Habitus-corrected length tapes outperform Broselow for resuscitation weight estimates

A systematic review and meta-analysis of 250 studies, nine in children actually needing resuscitation, compared tools for estimating weight and calculating drug doses. The PAWPER tape, a length-based tape with body-habitus correction, outperformed the Broselow tape for time to drug administration, dose accuracy and weight accuracy (odds ratio 2.48 for estimates within 10%). Age-based formulas were consistently inaccurate. No study reported patient outcomes.

  1. 1

    Empirical tuberculosis treatment in infants living with HIV hospitalised with severe pneumonia in Africa (EMPIRICAL): a multicentre, randomised, open-label, 2 × 2 factorial, controlled superiority trial

    Researchers in Côte d'Ivoire, Malawi, Mozambique, Uganda, Zambia and Zimbabwe ran a multicentre open-label 2 × 2 factorial superiority randomised trial in 19 hospitals, enrolling 563 infants aged 28-365 days (558 analysed). It asked whether empirical tuberculosis treatment helps infants living with HIV hospitalised with severe pneumonia. Death by 12 months was 48% (132/276) with empirical TB treatment and 43% (121/282) without (adjusted HR 1.12, 95% CI 0.87-1.43; p=0.39). In the comparison groups, 46% started TB treatment anyway.

    The Lancet · 12 Nov 2026 (DOI registered; issue dated 14 Nov) · Read the paper

    Evidence and limitations
    Study
    Multicentre open-label 2 × 2 factorial superiority RCT; 563 infants aged 28-365 days enrolled, 558 analysed. Côte d'Ivoire, Malawi, Mozambique, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe (19 hospitals).
    Findings in full
    Death by 12 months was 48% (132/276) with empirical TB treatment and 43% (121/282) without (adjusted HR 1.12, 95% CI 0.87-1.43; p=0.39); serious adverse events did not differ. In the comparison groups, 46% started TB treatment anyway, at a median of 4 days.

No level 1 evidence existed for any domain.
    Why it matters
    The first international guidance for a rare, serious childhood disease; shared definitions make registries and trials possible across rheumatology, neurology, radiology and genetics.
    Limitations
    Consensus-based with no level 1 evidence; European-network led; effect on outcomes untested.

    Incidence and predictors of suboptimal functional outcomes despite successful reperfusion in paediatric stroke: a cohort study from the Save ChildS Pro registry

    Researchers from 45 centres in 12 countries analysed prospectively collected Save ChildS Pro registry data in a retrospective cohort of 101 children aged 28 days to 18 years with successful thrombectomy recanalisation. It is the first description of futile recanalisation in children. 18% had a suboptimal 90-day outcome (mRS >2), against 40-60% reported in adults. Lower final reperfusion grade (adjusted OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.23-0.84) and younger age (adjusted OR 0.87 per year) were associated with it. The analysis was exploratory.

How this was compiled

What was searched, how attention was measured, and what could not be checked.

  • This roundup is compiled for PVJ with the help of automated search and summarising tools, from published abstracts and database records. It is not peer reviewed.
  • The headlines in the first list are PVJ's plain-language wording, not the papers' titles; each paper's own title is under "The paper and its attention". The pictures are icons for the clinical area, not images from the papers.
  • Papers were found through PubMed and Europe PMC records for 7 to 13 November 2026, Crossref, the JAMA Network and Lancet online-first lists, and news and press-release searches. Titles, dates and findings come from those records and abstracts, not from full texts.
  • Altmetric Attention Scores were read from Altmetric's public pages on the evening of 13 November 2026. They change daily, and most papers only a few days old have no score yet, which is not the same as a score of zero.
  • Each list holds at most five JAMA Network papers and draws on at least five journals and five specialties. That cap kept six higher-scoring JAMA Network papers out of the in-focus list.
  • The notes under each paper say where a detail comes from a news report or press release instead of the paper itself. Where a paper's abstract disagrees with its own results, the figures are left out.
  • Papers published on 12 and 13 November may be missing because databases lag by a day or two. NEJM, The BMJ, Archives of Disease in Childhood and BMJ Paediatrics Open had nothing suitable this week.
