KEY POINTS
- An East of England team identified barriers to underserved communities being involved in paediatric genomic testing, and possible solutions.
- There are challenges to involvement at every stage of the patient pathway, from referral and consenting to participation and research reporting.
- Researchers should report the demographic characteristics of their populations so that this can be analysed further.
TRANSCRIPT
So I've worked as part of an East of England team looking at the involvement of underserved communities in genomic testing and paediatrics. So we've basically identified what the barriers are to these underserved communities being involved and tried to identify solutions to them as well.

The main thing that we found is that there are challenges to their involvement at every stage of the patient pathway, and that's from referral to consenting, participation in genomic testing programmes and with research reporting. And our key messages to people looking at genomic testing research as well would be to report demographic characteristics of their populations so that we can analyse this further. Think about the barriers facing patients when they are trying to encourage participation in genomic testing research as well, and try and leverage the solutions that we've identified as well to try and improve this.
