KEY POINTS
- Short digital stories and infographics are used to raise awareness of medical research among patients and the wider community.
- Sharing these on social media allows feedback to be collected from patients and their families about their views of this type of research.
- That feedback is taken to conferences to show medical professionals the appetite for integrating such treatments into standard care across the UK.
TRANSCRIPT
So I'm here at the Royal College today not as a clinical practitioner but rather for my engagement with medical research. I'm currently working on some really exciting initiatives focused on communicating some of the medical research we have at Brighton and Sussex Medical School to the patients and the wider community. And we're exploring the use of creative digital media, like short digital stories and engaging infographics, that we can share on social media to try and increase awareness about the medical research and how it can benefit patients and their families.

Also, by conducting these sorts of activities, we can then collect feedback from patients and their families regarding their views, their opinions of this type of research and the benefits that it can have. So we can then take that data to conferences such as this and we can show medical professionals what the appetite is for integrating those types of treatments into standard care. And hopefully with sharing that research with all of the medical professionals, they can go on to their local practices and we can gather the support necessary to integrate those treatments so they're accessible for patients and the wider community all across the UK.
