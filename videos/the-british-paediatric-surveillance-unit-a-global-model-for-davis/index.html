The British Paediatric Surveillance Unit: A Global Model for Rare Disease Research in Children

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 23 June 2025
  • 0:52 min
  • PD
    Dr Peter DavisChair of the British Paediatric Surveillance Unit (BPSU)

Dr Peter Davis, Chair of the British Paediatric Surveillance Unit (BPSU), discusses the pioneering role of the BPSU in advancing the understanding of rare diseases in children. Established in 1986, the BPSU was created to study rare diseases, determine their incidence and prevalence, and assess outcomes for affected children. Over more than three decades, the BPSU has become a model for similar surveillance units worldwide, with Germany following in 1992, and Australia, New Zealand, and Canada subsequently establishing their own units. These international collaborations have generated a wealth of studies and publications, significantly enhancing global knowledge about rare diseases in children. Dr Davis highlights the BPSU’s ongoing commitment to supporting research, sharing best practices, and fostering international partnerships to improve outcomes for children with rare diseases. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.
