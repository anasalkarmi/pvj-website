The Dr Me Project: student led workshops in empowering self-care in primary schools

  • RCPCH Conference 2024
  • Birmingham
  • Published 31 December 2024
  • 2:16 min
  • Ivy NgImperial College London

Key points

  • The Dr Me Project aims to improve health literacy in primary school children through education on self-care and appropriate use of NHS resources.
  • Medical school students delivered a 1-hour session with workshops on diarrhoea and vomiting, fever and sore throat, and head and minor injuries.
  • Correct answers to six case scenarios improved by an average of 26.3% after teaching, most in the diarrhoea and vomiting scenarios.

Ivy Ng, Imperial College London, discusses her work on 'The Dr Me Project' which aims to improve health literacy and education in primary school children. A group of students delivered an hour session to year 5 classes incorporating workshops on diarrhoea and vomiting, fever and sore throats. The team found the session to be well received by the children, with improvement in ability to select appropriate NHS services. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2024 in Birmingham, UK.

So today I'll just be sharing about a healthcare initiative that we started known as the Dr Me Project. So A&E attendances, over 20% of them for children under the age of 16 are classified as non-urgent and self-limiting conditions such as sore throat, coughs and [unclear], cost the NHS more than 2 billion pounds per year. With the increasing demand in NHS services, helping people, educating them to self-care for minor conditions and reducing unnecessary medical care would be really critical for a sustainable healthcare system. So research has shown that healthcare intervention should really start early in schools.

So what the Dr Me Project actually is, it aims to improve health literacy in primary school children through structured education on self-care and appropriate use of NHS resources. It also aims to widen medicine participation in deprived areas. So seven medical school students delivered a 1-hour session to two year five primary school students, and the session incorporated three workshops including diarrhoea and vomiting, fever and sore throat, and head and minor injuries.

So six case scenarios were asked before and after the workshop and each student had to decide whether they would stay at home, go to the GP or attend A&E. Regarding the results, post teaching their correct responses improved by an average of 26.3%, with an improvement greatest seen in the diarrhoea and vomiting scenarios.

So in conclusion, what we believe is that patient focused education, even starting at school age, can improve health literacy and promote self-care by equipping students with the knowledge of self-care and also the skills to actually navigate healthcare system effectively. Whilst actually inspiring children to consider the medical career, we can actually help pave the path for our long-term sustainability of NHS services and for the long-term healthcare workforce.
