Antibiotic allergy documentation and referral in a university teaching hospital

  • RCPCH Conference 2024
  • Birmingham
  • Published 30 December 2024
  • 1:44 min
  • Eva WoodingRoyal Devon University Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust

Key points

  • A flash audit of paediatric assessment unit admissions found antibiotic allergies were checked and documented in over 90% of cases.
  • Over the one-week period, no one was referred appropriately to the antibiotic delabelling clinic.
  • An education programme has been introduced in the trust, and a re-audit is planned to see whether it made a difference.

Eva Wooding, Royal Devon University Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust, discusses her work in assessing the appropriate uptake of penicillin allergy delabelling services in a University Teaching Hospital. The team found that referral to the paediatric antibiotic allergy de-labelling clinic was poor with 0% of 'low risk' allergies being referred. Further work needs to be carried out in paediatric services to support de-labelling of antibiotic allergies as we know fewer than 10% of people that believe they have an antibiotic allergy really do.. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2024 in Birmingham, UK.

So we carried out a project which was a flash audit over a one-week period, looking at all of the admissions coming through our paediatric assessment unit. We specifically wanted to know whether someone had asked them if they had any allergies to antibiotics and whether they documented that, and we also wanted to know if they had been referred to our antibiotic delabelling clinic if they did have allergies to antibiotics.

So an antibiotic delabelling clinic is where we do a risk assessment based on the likelihood of a true allergy existing, and that's really important because if people have allergy labels but aren't really allergic to the antibiotic, then they're denied the opportunity to take the antibiotic that may be best for their infection. We also know that people who have antibiotic allergies are more likely to have problems with resistant organisms, and it causes problems for antimicrobial stewardship. It's actually really important.

And what we found was that in 100% of cases no one was referred appropriately during this one-week period to our antibiotic delabelling clinic, which is a real shame but gave us an educational opportunity which we've been working on. But we were checking antibiotic allergies in over 90% of cases and documenting that for each admission. So we were concerned to find that there weren't many people actually being referred on appropriately to our clinic.

So we used this flash audit as an opportunity to introduce a programme of education in our trust, and are then planning to re-audit that to see if we've made a difference, because we know that this clinic is a really effective way of delabelling patients, and we know that fewer than 10% of people who think they have an allergy to an antibiotic really do.
