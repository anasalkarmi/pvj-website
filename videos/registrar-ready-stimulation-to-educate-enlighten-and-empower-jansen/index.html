Registrar Ready: stimulation to educate, enlighten and empower

  • Birmingham

Key points

  • Registrar Ready sessions for trainees moving up to the tier 2 rota were previously ad hoc and variable, so the team organised them more consistently.
  • Each candidate does one simulation on the day and watches the others, with scenarios ranging from acute resuscitation to safeguarding and child sexual exploitation.
  • The aim is to show trainees they already have the skills and confidence, and the team hopes to bring it into the tier 2 readiness form on the e-portfolio.

Nat Jansen, Health Education England, discusses his work in trying to provide an equitable experience for trainees undertaking the Registrar Ready days in the North of England. These days are aimed at trainees prior to starting the second on-call (tier 2) rota. Previous sessions were delivered on an ad-hoc basis and were variable depending on the location within the region. The team aimed to organise the training days in a more succinct fashion and to deliver more precisely. A simulation based medical education was incorporated in the course which trainees found to be relevant and improve their confidence when they take responsibility in real life clinical situations. Standardising has meant that paediatric trainees have an equitable experience when attending the course. The authors hope to integrate this into regional recommendations as part of "readiness for tier 2 rota" e-portfolio entry. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2024 in Birmingham, UK.

So I'm here presenting Registrar Ready, Simulation to Educate, Enlighten and Empower, on behalf of myself, Liz [unclear] and John Williams, and working with Health Education [unclear]. Previously these sessions have been a bit ad hoc and were very variable dependent on the location within the region, and so we took it upon ourselves to try and organise this in a bit more of a succinct fashion and to deliver it more precisely.

So we've done that over the past few years, capturing as many trainees as possible and getting them to do real life simulations, each candidate doing one simulation on the day and watching the other candidates to learn from them as well. The simulations range from acute resuscitation scenarios such as SVT through to the more difficult nuanced communication of safeguarding or child sexual exploitation, and it's looking at both those clinical skills but also the human factors and what they need to do.

It's mainly about showing them that they've already got the skills and the confidence to go onto the tier 2 rota, rather than testing them and sort of putting them through their paces. And so we really are getting the faculty to really be encouraging, educating them and making it as friendly and open discussion afterwards as possible. And we've got some really good positive feedback from the candidates so far. As I say, it's now into its third iteration and it's evolving as we go, and we're hoping to bring it into the tier 2 readiness form on the e-portfolio as part of their progress.
