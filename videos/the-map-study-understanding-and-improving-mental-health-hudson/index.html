The MAP Study: Understanding and Improving Mental Health Admissions to Paediatric Wards

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 4 July 2025
  • 2:01 min
  • LH
    Dr Lee HudsonClinical Associate Professor, UCL Institute of Child Health; Consultant General Paediatrician, Great Ormond Street Hospital

Dr Lee Hudson, Clinical Associate Professor, UCL Institute of Child Health; Consultant General Paediatrician, Great Ormond Street Hospital, introduces the MAP study (Mental Health Admissions to the Paediatric Ward), an NIHR-funded project based at University College London. The study responds to a marked rise in children admitted to general paediatric wards for acute mental health crises—a trend with little existing literature. Over the past decade, there has been a 65% increase in such admissions in England, with a particularly dramatic 500% rise among 11–15-year-old girls with eating disorders. The project comprises three work packages: analysis of national trends (recently published in The Lancet Child & Adolescent Health), a surveillance study with 15 hospitals to gather granular data on admissions, and qualitative interviews with young people, families, and staff to identify areas for improvement. The findings are being synthesised with input from major stakeholders, including the RCPCH, the Royal College of Psychiatrists, young people, and parents, to develop recommendations for better care both in the UK and internationally. Dr Hudson emphasises the urgent need for improved approaches, as mental health admissions to paediatric wards are increasing and require better support for both patients and staff. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.
