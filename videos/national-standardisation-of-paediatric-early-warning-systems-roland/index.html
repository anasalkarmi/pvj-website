National standardisation of paediatric early warning systems and their impact on care

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 23 June 2025
  • 1:21 min
  • Professor Damian RolandConsultant in paediatric emergency medicine and honorary professor at Leicester Hospitals and University of Leicester

Key points

  • Variability and lack of consensus on paediatric early warning systems meant clinicians moving between hospitals sometimes talked about different things.
  • A national standardised PEWS has been released and 60% of hospitals are using it or putting it into action.
  • A pilot in emergency departments is under way, with the SPOT programme aiming to extend the system into the community.

Professor Damian Roland, consultant in paediatric emergency medicine and honorary professor at Leicester Hospitals and University of Leicester, provides insights into the development and implementation of national standardised paediatric early warning systems (PEWS) in the UK. The discussion highlights how, for many years, hospitals used a variety of PEWS, leading to confusion and inconsistency when clinicians moved between settings. In response, NHS England, the RCPCH, and the RCN collaborated to create a national program, resulting in the release of a standardised PEWS in late 2023. Currently, 60% of hospitals are either using the new system or are in the process of implementing it. Professor Roland also describes an exciting pilot program extending PEWS into emergency departments and, ultimately, into the wider community as part of the SPOT (Systemwide Paediatric Observation Tracking) initiative. This will enable all healthcare professionals to communicate about a child’s condition using a common language, improving safety and continuity of care. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.

So for a long time, hospitals have been using paediatric early warning scores and systems. Sadly though, because there was so much variability and no consensus on what the best particular model was, it meant that when people moved between hospitals or were communicating between hospitals, they were sometimes talking about different things.

So in 2018, it was that long ago, NHS England, the RCPCH and the RCN got together to create a programme of work for a national standardised paediatric early warning system that was released a year last November, and at this point in time 60% of hospitals are either using the national standardised PEWS or in the process of putting it into action.

In an exciting new turn, we've now looked at the emergency department use of PEWS and we've started a pilot programme which will hopefully come to fruition this summer. So, we will then have an inpatient PEWS. We will have an emergency department PEWS and then as part of the SPOT programme, systemwide paediatric observation tracking, we will move out into the community, prehospital, and hopefully then have a system of which any health care professional seeing a child will be able to communicate in the same language with anyone else.
