Recognising and diagnosing X-linked hypophosphataemia in paediatric practice

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 4 July 2025
  • 1:29 min
  • Dr Sanjay GuptaConsultant paediatrician at Hull University Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust

Key points

  • X-linked hypophosphataemia can present to dentists, neurosurgeons, orthopaedic surgeons and paediatricians, so a high index of suspicion matters.
  • In 20 to 30% of cases the condition arises from de novo mutations, so a family history may be absent.
  • It is most commonly misdiagnosed as nutritional rickets, and laboratories should use age- and sex-specific phosphate ranges rather than adult ones.

Dr Sanjay Gupta, consultant paediatrician at Hull University Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust, discusses the importance of recognising rare diseases such as X-linked hypophosphataemia (XLH) in children. He emphasises that while a family history may raise suspicion for this X-linked dominant condition, up to 20–30% of cases are due to de novo mutations and may present to a variety of healthcare professionals—including dentists (for dental abscesses), neurosurgeons (for head shape deformities), orthopaedic surgeons, and paediatricians. These practical tips aim to improve early recognition and accurate diagnosis of XLH, ultimately enhancing care for affected children. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.
