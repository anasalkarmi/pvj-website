KEY POINTS
- The Samson Gemmell chair at the University of Glasgow is the oldest university chair of paediatrics in the UK and turned 100 last year.
- Academic paediatrics is still around, and there are ways of making it a success even 100 years into the chair.
- Academic paediatrics should involve other disciplines and not only paediatricians, so the community collectively improves paediatric health.
TRANSCRIPT
Thanks so much for asking me to speak about the academic chair of child health coming of age today. You may not know this, but the Samson Gemmell chair of child health which I hold in the University of Glasgow is the oldest chair of paediatrics, the university chair of paediatrics in the UK, and it turned 100 years last year. For that, myself and two previous chairs who were in this post, Lawrence Weaver and Forester Kubern, we decided to write a little piece in the Archives of Disease in Childhood in honour of that 100 years centenary.

And the main message of that particular paper was very much emphasising that academic paediatrics was still around. And there are ways of making it a success even 100 years into the chair of paediatrics. So that was the main message.

How you do that, I think all centres in the UK have to find their own magic formula for that. But it has to be with interaction with a number of different disciplines and making sure that academic paediatrics is not just done by paediatricians, but there are a number of other people who can do the same research in that field of paediatrics and child health, so that as a collective community we are all improving paediatric health. And that was it really.
