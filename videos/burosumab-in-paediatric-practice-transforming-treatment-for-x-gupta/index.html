Burosumab in Paediatric Practice: Transforming Treatment for X-Linked Hypophosphataemia

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 4 July 2025
  • 1:20 min
  • SG
    Dr Sanjay GuptaConsultant paediatrician at Hull University Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust

Dr Sanjay Gupta discusses the novel treatment for X-linked hypophosphataemia (XLH), focusing on burosumab—a fully human monoclonal antibody that targets the underlying pathology of the disease. Traditionally, XLH has been managed with high doses of oral phosphate and activated vitamin D, treatments that are often challenging due to poor compliance, unpleasant taste, gastrointestinal side effects, and the need for daily administration. Burosumab, administered as a fortnightly subcutaneous injection, addresses the root cause by inhibiting excess fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF-23), which leads to phosphate loss in urine and reduced activated vitamin D levels. Dr Gupta shares his clinical experience, noting excellent tolerability in children treated with burosumab, normalisation of phosphate levels, improved bone health without the need for further corrective surgery, and significant reduction in bone pain—resulting in a marked improvement in quality of life. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.
