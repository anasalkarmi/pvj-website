Medical photography in child protection

  • RCPCH Conference 2025
  • Glasgow
  • Published 22 June 2025
  • 2:22 min
  • Dr Tapomay BanerjeeConsultant paediatrician with Cambridgeshire Community Services NHS Trust

Key points

  • A photograph tells much more than a written description of an injury mark, but not every team has access to medical photography.
  • An audit of 25 children's photographs found them stored in appropriate format and secure folders, with injuries and body parts identifiable.
  • Doctors found medical photography useful in peer review and for children's social care, despite some concerns about acceptability.

Dr Tapomay Banerjee, a consultant paediatrician with Cambridgeshire Community Services NHS Trust, provides insights into the implementation and impact of medical photography in child protection work within the Bedford community paediatric team. Dr Banerjee describes the findings of his study which highlights the importance of photographic documentation in child protection examinations, where photographs can convey more information than written descriptions alone. Dr Banerjee details the training received by the team, the audit of 25 children’s photographs, and the positive results regarding image quality, storage security, and the ability of clinicians to accurately identify injuries and body parts. He emphasises the utility of medical photography in peer review and collaboration with social care, while acknowledging ongoing concerns about data protection and acceptability. Dr Banerjee encourages other trusts to adopt similar pathways to enhance child protection practice. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.

They have been taken in right way and two of the authors have independently looked at all these images and they could identify the body part, they could identify the injury mark, and that clearly matches with the injury description.

The doctors who have done this photography, they found it extremely useful and there are some concerns obviously they have raised, that whether these are [unclear], whether they will be acceptable.
