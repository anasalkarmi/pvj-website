Lumbar puncture toolkit for a children's hospital

  • RCPCH Conference 2024
  • Birmingham
  • Published 30 December 2024
  • 2:29 min
  • Kosisochukwu NwigweSheffield Childrens' Hospital

Key points

  • A quality improvement project produced guidelines, a parent information leaflet and a lumbar puncture kit for a paediatric setting.
  • A survey found that some NICE guidelines on lumbar puncture were not being followed in the trust, in practice and in the equipment used.
  • Lumbar puncture kits are now in all clinical areas, and a re-audit is planned to check compliance with the guideline.

Kosisochukwu Nwigwe, Sheffield Childrens' Hospital, discusses his work in producing a lumbar puncture toolkit comprised of a departmental guideline, a patient information leaflet and a lumbar puncture box.

My name is Kosisochukwu Nwigwe. I am at the RCPCH conference today to present my quality improvement project. This project is called a lumbar puncture project, where we developed guidelines, a parent information leaflet and a lumbar puncture kit in a paediatric setting in the district general hospital, in this instance Sheffield Children's Hospital.

Lumbar puncture is an aseptic technique where [unclear] fluid is obtained from the lumbar space and it undergoes analysis, and this helps us to diagnose a lot of medical conditions, for example when you have meningitis, which is an infection of the meninges or the brain and whatnot.

In doing this quality improvement project, our aim was to find out about the knowledge that other clinicians have got in lumbar puncture. We had to send out Google forms, and this involved Google forms to the consultants, to the registrars and to other members of the team, to find out the knowledge they have about lumbar puncture. Surprisingly, some of the guidelines which have been stated by the NICE guidelines are not being followed in the trust. This goes ahead to involve what people do and also the equipment that people use. So part of my quality improvement project was to make out a way to standardise the care of lumbar puncture, that is to say to ensure that everyone is on the same level in doing lumbar punctures and any other process associated with it.

Following this project we were able to provide a lumbar puncture kit in all the clinical areas. This included the ED, the emergency department, and also in the postnatal and the neonatal unit. This helped our colleagues to ensure that lumbar puncture is being done with the right instrument, the right cleaning solutions are being used, and also that it is done in an aseptic procedure. About 90% of participants agreed that [unclear] consent is necessary, whereas others didn't see this as fit.

I hope in the future that we're going to do a re-audit of this to ensure that people comply to the guideline stated out by the NICE guideline, by ensuring that everyone uses the right equipment and people also use the right technique to ensure that lumbar puncture is being done safely in children. For example, when a lumbar puncture is being done wrongly it could lead to traumatic injury to the nerves in the babies, and this could have long-term complications. However, by merely educating people to use simple and small size needle, for example a 22G or 25G needle, will help to prevent this long-term complication.
