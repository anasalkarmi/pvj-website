KEY POINTS
- Paediatric pharmacy labels vary hugely, and how the dose is written, in milligrams, millilitres or both, causes the most confusion.
- The most important thing is that all labels are written the same way, but parents prefer the dose in millilitres.
- All liquid medicines should be written in millilitres to give the dose, and a position statement and talks with regulators are under way.
TRANSCRIPT
Whilst there's legal aspects to labelling, that is what must be on a pharmacy label, the detail of it actually isn't very specific, and as we've been looking at paediatric labels, we've realised that the variance on them is absolutely huge. Probably the biggest one and the one which creates the most problems is how the dose is written. Some people write it in milligrams, some people in millilitres, some in millilitres and milligrams, some in milligrams and millilitres and so on. And that causes all sorts of confusion.

Now we've tried to do some work to get the answer to what is the best way of labelling. In some ways it's not the important thing. The important thing is so that all labels are written the same way irrespective of how they're written. But we do know that parents do prefer it in millilitres. After all that is what they are going to give their medicine in. Therefore they need to know what it is they are drawing up.

And so we've done a lot of work around all sorts of aspects of labelling to try and get to a standard and the Neonatal and Paediatric Pharmacy Group are just doing a position statement on what those should be and we're talking to the regulators to move this on as well and hopefully these will become national standards for all pharmacy labels. And that primary thing is we believe that all liquid medicines should be written in millilitres to give the dose.
