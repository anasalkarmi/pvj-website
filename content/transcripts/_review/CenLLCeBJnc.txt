KEY POINTS
- Electronic records from 2018 to 2021 were used to profile paediatric patients diagnosed with TB at a large East London NHS trust.
- Pulmonary TB was by far the most common clinical phenotype, and there was a female predominance in a mainly South Asian population.
- There was no uniform testing method, and the diagnostic methods used differed between hospitals.
TRANSCRIPT
So my poster is about a profile of paediatric patients diagnosed with TB in a large East London NHS trust. We gathered our information by looking at the electronic records from the years 2018 to 2021. We were looking at things like the characteristics of the patients who were diagnosed with TB, the year-on-year admission rates, we looked at their diagnostic results including IGRA and Mantoux, and also the clinical phenotypes of their disease. Other things we looked at were their treatment time, which medication regimens they were treated with, and cases of multi-drug resistant TB as well.

So as a quick summary, just in terms of demographics of our patients, we noticed a female predominance in our mainly South Asian population. In terms of year-on-year admission rates there was a dip in 2020, which is consistent with going into lockdown during the Covid-19 pandemic and also less international travel. In terms of our clinical phenotype, pulmonary TB was the most common by far. Other diagnoses were lymphadenopathy and disseminated TB.

In terms of our diagnostic results, we noticed some concordance between Mantoux and IGRA, but what really highlighted to us was that there wasn't a uniform testing method used in terms of which diagnostic methods were used, and I think this differs between hospital to hospital. In terms of multidrug resistant TB there was only one case, but overall we had hosal right up 100%.
