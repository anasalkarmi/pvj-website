KEY POINTS
- The Grenfell Tower response differed from other major incidents in the nature of injuries, the psychological impact on staff and the high number of children and families.
- Working under pressure in the NHS affects staff generally, so wellbeing support starts with the basics such as rota, leave and education.
- Schwartz Rounds and a positive reporting system let staff reflect on emotional cases and show gratitude and recognition to each other.
TRANSCRIPT
So my talk was about mass casualty events, but specifically about my experience of being involved in the Grenfell Tower fire response. I unexpectedly became the ED consultant lead for the response that night. I happened to be doing a trauma night shift when the major incident was declared. And the first part of the talk is the synopsis of the events that night. How I felt real anxiety at being thrown into it unexpectedly. How the response went from an operational side.

And then also how the Grenfell Tower response was very different from other major incidents we'd been involved in. We'd have been involved in the Westminster Bridge, the London Bridge terrorist attacks. They were very different in their nature, in the nature of the burden of injury that we dealt with, but also in terms of the psychological impact on staff, the nature of the patients that we dealt with, the high number of children and families also that came in through that night.

I then reflected on how that impacted me personally, and that did have a real emotional cost, and my journey in sort of processing that and being able to move on. But then it got me thinking about how major incidents, and not just major incidents but working in the pressure of the NHS, how that impacts our staff generally, and how we can better support our colleagues' wellbeing and build resilience within ourselves but also amongst our colleagues.

So I then spoke about wellbeing initiatives that we've put into place, starting with the kind of essential, what I call the bricks and mortar of wellbeing, things like rota, leave, education etc. But then focusing in a little bit more detail about two particular initiatives, one being Schwartz Rounds, which are reflective rounds around the emotional impact of dealing with a particular case. And then secondly also a positive reporting system, which a lot of trusts call the [unclear] system, where we built, put together a process where staff could show appreciation to each other, give gratitude and recognition of acts of kindness, compassion, but also great performance that go above and beyond.

And that for me was the most important learning from Grenfell, was how we can actually support our staff, because actually we have to remember that we're not just technically very proficient. We're not just highly trained in a clinical sense, but we are in a caring profession and we have to be able to show compassion to our patients, but to do that we have to be compassionate to ourselves, role model compassion and be compassionate to our colleagues. And that essentially was my talk.
