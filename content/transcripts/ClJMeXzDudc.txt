KEY POINTS
- A review of over 300 patient notes compared asthma and allergy outcomes and A&E attendances with ethnicity and socioeconomic status.
- Certain ethnic groups were more likely to have more severe asthma on an asthma severity scale and more likely to come into A&E.
- Next steps are to look at barriers to accessing asthma and allergy clinics and at parents and carers experiences.
TRANSCRIPT
I'm looking at asthma and allergy in children and young people. I did a retrospective service evaluation and looked at over 300 patient notes and looked at clinical outcomes and A&E attendances and compared that to ethnicity and socioeconomic status based on the postcode the child lived in.

And we found that there were differences in clinical outcomes. We found that certain ethnic groups were more likely to have more severe asthma based on an asthma severity scale. And we also found that they were more likely to come into A&E.

So it's only the start of my project. The next steps will be looking at what barriers there are to children accessing asthma allergy clinics and parents and carers' experiences of looking after a child with asthma allergy.
