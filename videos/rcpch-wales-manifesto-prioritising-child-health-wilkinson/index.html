Key points

  • RCPCH Wales published a manifesto for the 2026 Senedd elections, citing a significant deterioration in child health and wellbeing.

  • The four priorities are empowering the workforce, transforming services, embracing data and digital innovation, and tackling inequalities.

Dr Nick Wilkinson, Officer for Wales at the Royal College of Paediatrics and Child Health (RCPCH), and Dr Dana Beasley, Deputy Officer for Wales, discuss the launch of the RCPCH Wales manifesto in anticipation of the 2026 Senedd elections. They highlight the significant deterioration in the health and well-being of children and young people in Wales, marked by rising obesity, worsening mental health, and widening health inequalities. The paediatric workforce is described as tired, stretched, and at risk of burnout due to chronic underinvestment and lack of funding, resulting in some of the poorest child health outcomes in Europe. The manifesto outlines four core priorities: empowering the child health workforce, transforming children’s health services, embracing data and digital innovation, and tackling inequalities to deliver for all children. Dr Wilkinson and Dr Beasley call on all politicians to commit to the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child and invite engagement to help transform children’s services in Wales. This interview took place at the RCPCH conference 2025 in Glasgow, Scotland.

Hi, welcome to the conference for the Royal College of Paediatrics and Child Health. My name is Nick Wilkinson. I'm the Officer for Wales.

Hello there. My name is Dana Beasley and I'm the Deputy Officer for Wales.

As RCPCH Wales, we have published our manifesto in view of the upcoming elections in the Senedd in 2026. We notice significant deterioration in the health and wellbeing of our children and young people in Wales. As paediatricians, we are aware there are rising levels of obesity, rising levels of mental health issues, and there's widening health inequalities. But we also know that our workforce is tired, is stretched, and at the risk of burnout. There's a distinct lack of investment and funding of child health, and as a result, our children have the poorest health outcomes within Europe.

So, we've come up with four distinct priorities. This includes empowering the child health workforce, transforming children's health services, embracing data and digital innovation, and then delivering for all by tackling the inequalities in child health. To empower this, we're calling on all politicians to commit to the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child. And we look forward to engaging with you all to help transform children's services in Wales.
